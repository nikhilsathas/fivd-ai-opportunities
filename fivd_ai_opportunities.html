Data Centre AI opportunities: priority and fit

This view brings together 61 AI use cases across the data centre lifecycle. Each use case is scored on the value it could create for an owner and whether a credible pilot can be delivered now. Start with the priority map and the calculation behind it, describe an owner profile in the Fit finder, or read the business stories the use cases group into.

Priority map and full list

Each use case is scored on Value and Implementability. Value measures the scale of the owner outcome. Implementability measures whether a credible pilot can be delivered now. The priority score is 45 per cent Value and 55 per cent Implementability. Both axes are published separately, so a different weighting can be applied to the same scores.

The method has three steps. Each factor below is scored from 1 to 5 against a fixed definition. The factor weights within an axis produce one score out of 100 for that axis. Priority is then 0.45 × Value plus 0.55 × Implementability. Every input and every weight is listed here and in the workbook, so any score on the list can be traced back to the five judgements behind it and recalculated under different weights.

Scores are shown as whole numbers, because a manual 1 to 5 judgement does not support a decimal. Differences under two points are a group and not an order. Impl. is Implementability.

Value and Implementability are the two coordinates on the plot. The rank combines both, and the quadrant shows which coordinate is driving the result.

Fit finder

Select the owner profile and the site conditions. The tool shows the use cases that best fit the profile and explains why others are excluded. The rules are directional and should be confirmed with FivD.
Owner profile
Who is accountable for the decision, and what they sell. Each use case names the owner types it was written for.
Site or estate size
A proxy for whether the problem is large enough to fund the work. Most use cases carry a size floor, and below it the saving is smaller than the effort.
Lifecycle
Whether the decision happens before concrete or during operations. The two have different owners and different data, and this moves the most use cases.
High-density conversion underway
Whether racks are being pushed past what the existing cooling and power design was built for. Eight use cases are about that conversion itself.
GPU fleet
Who controls the accelerators. Eleven use cases need a fleet to exist, and seven of those need the owner to operate it, because that is a question of permission to act.
Portfolio structure
Whether there is anything to compare against. Forty-three use cases draw their value from comparing across sites or reusing a finding on the next project.
Business story
Optionally focus the results on one owner outcome. This narrows the use cases shown without changing their fit assessment or priority.

Every answer maps to a condition recorded against the use case: the owner profiles it suits, the size band below which it stops mattering, the lifecycle stage it applies at, and whether it depends on a density conversion, a GPU fleet or several sites. Where the profile is missing a condition, the use case moves to the lower fit list with the reason shown.

Two further conditions are recorded in the workbook and are no longer asked here, because they apply to only a handful of use cases: a constrained grid position or interconnection queue, and standby generation on site. Where a use case depends on one of them, the card says so, so the condition is checked in conversation rather than assumed.

The output is a directional fit and not a recommendation. Fit rules last reviewed on 17 August 2026. The fit rules are directional and will be validated with FivD subject-matter experts.
